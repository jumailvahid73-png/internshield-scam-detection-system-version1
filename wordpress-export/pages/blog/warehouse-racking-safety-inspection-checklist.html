Warehouse Racking Safety: A Practical Inspection Checklist

Home/Blog/Warehouse Racking Safety: A Practical Inspection Checklist
July 5, 2026 4 min read Orient Tech Team

Pallet racking fails quietly. A dented upright or a missing safety pin rarely causes a problem the same day it happens -- which is exactly why routine inspection matters more than reacting to visible damage. Here's a practical checklist to run through every quarter.

Uprights and frames

  • Check for dents, bends or visible impact damage, especially at forklift height
  • Confirm base plates are flush with the floor and anchor bolts are tight
  • Look for rust or corrosion on frames in humid or outdoor-adjacent storage areas

Beams and connectors

  • Verify safety locking pins are present on every beam-to-frame connection
  • Check for visible sagging or deflection under load
  • Confirm beam levels match the load they're currently carrying

Load and signage

  • Confirm load capacity signage is present, legible and matches the current configuration
  • Spot-check that stored loads don't exceed rated capacity per beam level or bay
  • Check that pallets are stored square and aren't overhanging the beam edge

Aisles and access

  • Confirm aisle widths are still adequate for the forklifts currently in use
  • Check that column protectors are in place at aisle entrances and corners
  • Make sure nothing is stored in a way that blocks fire exits or sprinkler access

When to call in a professional inspection

A visual quarterly check by warehouse staff is a good baseline, but it isn't a substitute for a formal racking safety inspection. If you've had a forklift collision, if the system is more than a few years old, or if you simply can't remember the last inspection, it's worth having a professional assessment before it becomes a bigger issue.
